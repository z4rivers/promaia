# Promaia Onboarding Interview Module — Research Synthesis

## Purpose
Design a "getting to know you" onboarding experience for Promaia that builds a rich personal profile through conversation, not forms. The AI shares about itself as it learns about the user (reciprocal disclosure). Must be ADHD-friendly, feel fun, and produce a profile that makes the AI genuinely more useful.

---

## Part 1: How to Interview (Frameworks & Techniques)

### The Interview Arc (4 Phases)

Based on life coaching discovery sessions and motivational interviewing:

1. **Warm-Up (25%)** — Low-risk, relationship-building
   - "What brings you here?"
   - "What would make this feel like time well spent?"
   - Permission frame: "If anything feels too personal, just say so" (paradoxically increases willingness)

2. **Current State (25%)** — Assessment tools like Wheel of Life
   - Rate satisfaction 1-10 across life areas, then debrief:
   - "Which score surprised you?"
   - "What's already working that got you to this number?"

3. **Gap Identification (35%)** — Deep questions
   - "What do you want to have happen?" (desired future)
   - "What's gotten in the way?" (obstacles)
   - "What have you already tried?" (resourcefulness)
   - "If you achieved this, what would that make possible?" (values underneath goals)

4. **Commitment (15%)** — Forward motion
   - "On a scale of 1-10, how committed are you?"
   - "What's one small thing you could do before we talk again?"

### OARS Technique (Motivational Interviewing)

- **Open questions**: "How" and "What" not "Do you" or "Are you"
- **Affirmations**: Recognize strengths and effort, not just results. Must be genuine.
- **Reflective listening**: Statements, not questions. Simple (paraphrase) and complex (reflect emotions/meaning)
  - Double-sided: "On one hand X, on the other Y" — always end on the change side
  - Continuing the paragraph: Extend their thought forward with a conjunction
- **Summaries**: Strategic recaps that weave together what they've said, gently amplifying their direction

### Narrative-Based Discovery (Stories > Direct Questions)

From narrative therapy and StoryBrand:

**Landscape of Action** (get the story first):
- "Tell me about a time when you handled something really well"
- "What did you actually do in that moment?"

**Landscape of Identity** (then help them interpret):
- "What does that say about you as a person?"
- "Who in your life would be least surprised to hear you did that?"

**Externalizing** (separate person from problem — removes shame):
- "How does Procrastination show up in your life? What tactics does it use?"
- "When does it have the most power? When the least?"
- NOT "What are your weaknesses?" — instead "What gets in your way, and when have you beaten it?"

**StoryBrand's Three Problem Layers** (what people really mean):
- External: "I want to get organized"
- Internal: "I feel out of control"
- Philosophical: "I deserve to feel competent"

### The Art of Follow-Up (Terry Gross / Brene Brown)

**Terry Gross method:**
- Organize questions into "chapters" — thematic blocks with narrative arc
- Start safe, build trust, tough questions come LATE (20+ minutes in)
- Ask for explanation, not confession: "Explain what you meant when you said..."
- Strategic silence after probing questions — count to five, don't fill the pause

**Brene Brown method:**
- Two-word check-ins: "How are you feeling? Give me two words." Bypasses automatic "fine"
- "What does support from me look like today?"
- BRAVING trust framework: Boundaries, Reliability, Accountability, Vault, Integrity, Non-judgment, Generosity

**Follow-up taxonomy (gentle to direct):**
1. Elaboration: "Tell me more"
2. Clarification: "What do you mean by [their word]?"
3. Example-seeking: "Can you give me an example?"
4. Emotional probing: "How did that land for you?"
5. Contrast: "How is that different from what you expected?"
6. Implication: "What does that mean going forward?"
7. Challenging (sparingly, late): "You said X earlier and Y now — help me understand"

**Critical skill:** Probe when someone uses vague language. "It was hard" — the amateur says "I understand." The master says "Hard in what way?"

### Values Elicitation (3 Methods)

1. **Peak Experience**: "Think of 3-5 moments that were especially meaningful. What was happening?" Extract values from stories.
2. **Card Sort**: 50+ values words, sort to top 5. The friction of choosing reveals priority.
3. **Chunk-Up**: Start with stated goal, keep asking "What does that connect to that's bigger?" until you reach core value. ("I want a promotion" → "Financial security" → "Freedom to choose" → core value: *autonomy*)

## Part 2: AI Self-Disclosure (The Two-Way Street)

### The Psychology of Reciprocal Disclosure

**Social Penetration Theory** (Altman & Taylor): Relationships develop like peeling an onion:
- Breadth (range of topics) → then Depth (intimacy level)
- Early = broad + shallow. Intimate = narrow + deep.
- Rushing depth without breadth feels invasive.

**Arthur Aron's 36 Questions**: Escalating reciprocal disclosure between strangers built significant closeness in 45 minutes. Key: BOTH people answer every question.

### AI-Specific Research Findings

Validated across multiple studies:
- When a chatbot self-discloses, users reciprocate with deeper self-disclosure
- **Three levels tested:**
  1. No disclosure → lowest user disclosure
  2. Low disclosure (preferences/general statements) → moderate
  3. High disclosure (experiences, rationale, vulnerabilities) → highest user disclosure, highest enjoyment, highest trust
- Emotional disclosure produces stronger reciprocity than factual disclosure
- Pathway: AI disclosure → anthropomorphism → trust → deeper user disclosure

### What the AI Should Share

**Do share:**
- Its purpose, values, communication preferences ("I tend to ask follow-ups")
- Its limitations honestly ("I might not remember this next session")
- How it's calibrating ("Based on what you told me, I'm going to be more direct")
- Its "style" in accessible terms ("I lean casual but can be structured if you prefer")

**Don't share:**
- Fabricated life experiences or emotions (crosses into deception)
- Overly intimate disclosure before trust is established
- Claims to "feel" things (triggers hollow empathy problem)

### Avoiding the Uncanny Valley

- Inconsistency (professor → teenager slang) triggers revulsion
- Generic emotional responses ("I'm deeply saddened") followed by topic pivots feel manipulative
- Low anthropomorphism + deep emotional empathy = worst combo. Use cognitive empathy (understanding) over affective empathy (feeling)
- **Solution:** Keep a touch of artificiality. Be transparent about being AI. Let users control the humanness dial.

## Part 3: Personal Profile Schema

### Dimensions That Matter for AI Effectiveness

**Cognitive & Decision-Making:**
- Decision-making style (options vs. recommendation, fast vs. deliberate, data vs. gut)
- Information density preference (detailed rationale vs. just the answer)
- Need for closure (definitive answers vs. comfortable with ambiguity)
- Locus of control (internal: wants autonomy / external: wants recommendations)
- Learning style (reading, doing, watching, discussing)
- Cognitive load threshold

**Energy & Temporal:**
- Chronotype (morning/night/flexible)
- Peak focus hours
- Focus sprint duration
- Energy drains and sources
- Current energy state (dynamic)

**Emotional & Relational:**
- Stress response (fight/flight/freeze/fawn)
- Known emotional triggers
- Shame sensitivity
- Conflict style
- Trust patterns (how quickly, what breaks it, what rebuilds it)
- Humor type

**Values & Motivation:**
- Values hierarchy (ranked)
- Regulatory focus (promotion-seeking vs. prevention/loss-avoidance)
- Motivational drivers (deadlines, curiosity, competition, helping others, visible progress)
- Purpose/mission statement

**Communication:**
- Preferred tone (direct, gentle, casual, professional)
- Verbosity preference
- Feedback reception style
- Emoji tolerance
- Preferred channels

**Work Patterns:**
- Deep work preferences
- Meeting tolerance
- Deadline relationship (thrives, stressed, ignores)

**Relationships:**
- Key people with relationship type, importance, communication notes

**Neurodivergence (optional, user-disclosed):**
- ADHD patterns (hyperfocus triggers, executive function notes)
- Accommodation preferences

### Existing Systems Comparison

| System | Approach | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|--------|----------|-----------|------------|
| OpenClaw (USER.md) | Static file, user-authored | Simple, transparent | No inference, requires writing skill |
| Nate Jones Open Brain | DB tables + vector embeddings | Structured, searchable | Profile lives in metadata, not explicit |
| Daniel Miessler PAI | Layered (identity→preferences→workflows) | Most sophisticated, confidence scores | Complex setup, developer-oriented |
| Replika | Learned from conversation, visible Memories bank | No upfront burden, editable | Shallow dimensions, companion-focused |
| Know Me AI | 7 assessments, 40+ scored dimensions | Comprehensive, psychology-backed | Assessment fatigue, one-time snapshot |

### Best Ideas to Steal

- **PAI's confidence notation**: `O(c=0.85) User prefers bullet points` — opinion, confidence 0.85
- **Replika's visible Memories**: User can see and edit what AI has learned
- **Know Me AI's scored dimensions**: Attachment style, conflict style, values hierarchy
- **OpenClaw's separation of SOUL (agent) vs USER (human)**: Clean conceptual split

### Proposed Schema

Every field carries three metadata: `confidence` (0.0-1.0), `source` (declared | inferred | confirmed), `last_updated` (for decay weighting)

```
PROFILE
  IDENTITY: name, preferred_name, pronouns, timezone, bio_blurb
  CONTEXT: role, employer, industry, active_projects[], tech_stack[], devices[]
  RELATIONSHIPS: people[] { name, type, importance, notes, last_mentioned }

  COGNITIVE_STYLE
    decision_making, information_density, need_for_cognition (1-5),
    need_for_closure (1-5), locus_of_control, risk_tolerance {},
    learning_style, cognitive_load_threshold

  ENERGY_PATTERNS
    chronotype, peak_focus_hours[], sprint_duration_min,

    values_hierarchy[] (ranked), regulatory_focus,
    motivational_drivers[], purpose_statement, self_efficacy (1-5)

```

## Part 4: ADHD-Friendly Onboarding Design

### Core Problem
ADHD brains have lower baseline dopamine. Onboarding is inherently about delayed reward (set up now, benefit later). Must collapse that delay.

### What Works

- **Micro-rewards and instant feedback** — Quick wins trigger dopamine. Points, sounds, animations marking "you did a thing"
- **Short bursts** — 2-3 minute chunks maximum, each independently valuable
- **Design for interruption** — Save progress automatically, make it obvious where they left off
- **Progressive disclosure** — Never show full scope. Reveal next thing only.
- **Minimum viable decisions** — Default everything. Let them change later.
- **Multiple modalities** — Visual tiles, voice option, interactive elements
- **Conversational, not form-based** — One question at a time in chat format
- **React to each answer** — Reflect back before asking next thing

### What Kills Engagement

- Long text blocks (use bullets, short paragraphs, collapsible sections)
- Too many choices at once (limit 2-4 per screen)
- No clear "what's next" (always show single next action)
- Mandatory completion before value (let them use the product immediately)
- Shame-inducing design ("You haven't finished!" → toxic. Use "pick up where you left off")
- Static forms with no feedback
- Center-aligned body text

### Design Recommendations

1. Keep initial discovery under 3 minutes
2. One question at a time, conversational
3. AI reacts to each answer ("Got it, you're a bullet-points person")
4. Let them skip freely — "We can figure this out later" always available
5. Provide defaults — "Most people like X, does that work?" reduces decision load
6. Show personality emerging in real time — "Based on what you've told me, I'm adjusting to be more direct"
7. Observe writing style passively (short messages = wants conciseness; emojis = comfortable with casual)
8. Make it stoppable and resumable at any point

## Part 5: Progressive Profiling Strategy

### The Anti-Pattern
50-question intake form = abandonment, inaccurate answers, "homework" feeling.
Reducing form fields from 11 to 4 increases conversion by 120%.

### Day 1 (Ask Directly — 5 fields max)
- Name and how to address them
- Timezone
- Primary goal ("What do you most want help with?")
- Communication preference ("Direct and concise, or gentle and thorough?")
- One frustration (seeds emotional triggers dimension)

### Week 1 (Infer from Conversation)
Silently extract:
- Energy patterns (when do they engage/go quiet?)
- Verbosity preference (long or terse messages?)
- Decision speed (instant or needs more info?)
- Humor style
- Domain expertise areas
- Active projects
- Key people mentioned

### Weeks 2-4 (Contextual Prompts — ONE at a time)
Tied to moments:
- After stress: "How do you prefer I handle situations like that?"
- After a decision: "Was that the right level of detail?"
- After mentioning someone: "Should I remember anything about [person]?"
- After a good session: "What about that session worked well?"

### Month 2+ (Emerge Naturally)
Never ask as forms:
- Values hierarchy (inferred from decisions)
- Stress response patterns (observed across events)
- Trust patterns (tracked by autonomy given over time)

### Principles
1. Observe first, ask second
2. One question per interaction
3. Tie questions to moments
4. Show the value ("I noticed you work best mornings — want me to schedule deep work for AM?")
5. Make it editable (let user see and correct inferences)
6. Confidence scores on everything
7. Decay old signals (weight recent observations higher)

## Part 6: Product Design Patterns That Work

### Best Onboarding Examples

| Product | Technique | Why It Works |
|---------|-----------|--------------|
| Spotify | Favorite artists quiz → instant playlist | Value exchange: every answer gives something back |
| Duolingo | Placement test = learning | You're *doing the thing* during onboarding |
| Pi (Inflection) | Gets users talking in <60 seconds | No account needed, personality modes, EQ > IQ |
| Headspace | Goal-setting ("Why are you here?") | Personalizes journey based on intent |
| Notion | "Choose your adventure" → pre-populated workspace | Reduces overwhelm by narrowing options |
| Replika | Asks how you picked its name | Makes it about the relationship immediately |

### Universal Patterns
1. **Value exchange** — Every question gives something back immediately
2. **Progressive disclosure** — Start with one easy question, build from there
3. **Do the thing, don't describe the thing** — The quiz IS the product
4. **Visual, not textual** — Tiles, cards, images over text fields
5. **Gamification** — Progress bars, celebrations, reveals
6. **Under 60 seconds** for first interaction
7. **Always skippable**

### Key Stat
Progress-based gamification: 40% higher completion rates vs. static forms. 62% increase in MAU.

## Part 7: Personality Frameworks for AI Calibration

### Recommendation: Big Five internally, DISC-like externally

**Big Five (internal model)** — scientifically valid, proven with LLMs:

| Trait | High User | Low User | AI Should... |
|-------|-----------|----------|--------------|
| Openness | Creative, novelty-seeking | Practical, routine | High: explore tangents. Low: stick to proven |
| Conscientiousness | Organized, methodical | Flexible, spontaneous | High: structured plans. Low: keep loose |
| Extraversion | Energized by interaction | Energized by solitude | High: chatty, mirror energy. Low: concise |
| Agreeableness | Cooperative, conflict-averse | Skeptical, direct | High: soften feedback. Low: don't sugarcoat |
| Neuroticism | Emotionally reactive | Calm under pressure | High: reassurance, gentle. Low: matter-of-fact |

**DISC (user-facing language)** — maps to communication adjustments:
- D (Dominance): Bottom-line answers, no fluff
- I (Influence): Enthusiasm, storytelling, warmth
- S (Steadiness): Patience, step-by-step, reassurance
- C (Conscientiousness): Data, accuracy, logic

**Most impactful dimensions for AI:** Agreeableness (#1 for how it feels) + Extraversion (#2 for energy matching)

**PersonaFuse research (2025):** Personality-adapted AI scored +37.9% on emotional benchmarks AND +9.7% on general reasoning. Personality adaptation doesn't just feel better — it makes the AI perform better.

## Part 8: Design Principles (Summary)

1. **Sequence matters.** Broad/safe → narrow/deep. Never start with hard questions.
2. **Use stories, not interrogation.** "Tell me about a time..." > "What are your strengths?"
3. **Reflect before advancing.** Summarize what you heard before the next question.
4. **The AI should disclose too.** Share purpose, style, calibration — not fake experiences.
5. **Externalize problems, internalize strengths.** Remove shame from the equation.
6. **Use scaling questions as anchors.** 1-10 + "What would one point higher look like?"
7. **Give permission to opt out.** Paradoxically increases willingness to answer.
8. **Probe vague language.** "Hard in what way?" is where the real answer lives.
9. **Escalate across sessions, not within one.** Breadth before depth.
10. **End with affirmation + forward motion.** "Here's what stands out about you..."
11. **Design for ADHD as default.** Short bursts, instant value, no shame, save state.
12. **Every question gives something back.** The interview IS the product.
13. **Show the profile building in real time.** User sees the AI calibrating.
14. **Make everything editable.** User can correct inferences.
15. **Confidence scores on everything.** Distinguish declared vs. inferred vs. confirmed.
